## TIER 2 · BEYOND OUR EYES — we could not detect these being wrong

This is the list that matters, and it is not a list of missing *data*.

### 2.1 · The whole degree shape is conditional on an event that has not happened
Every number rests on `plan_model.ITEMS`, and that ledger assumes **a double major is
obtained**: ME is 18 credits rather than 24, `DM` is 12 units, `FREE` is the 15-credit residual
of that arithmetic. Admission is a **December, competitive decision made on Semesters 1–2 GPA**
— which this very semester determines.

So the recommendation is optimised for a degree shape that is not confirmed, by a process this
semester feeds. If admission fails, ME → 24 cr, `DM` vanishes, `FREE` changes, and every
deferral cost in R236 moves. **Nothing in the model represents the failure branch at all.**
`GPA_GATE_MULT` exists for exactly this and is set to 1.0, i.e. inert.

### 2.2 · Preference drift is untestable
Every schedule constant traces to something Iden said — that was the achievement of R221. But
the statements were elicited across five sessions in specific framings, and **R141 is a
recorded case of his answer moving when the framing changed**. Nothing re-tests whether the
preferences are still his. The model can be perfectly faithful to statements he would no longer
make, and it would look identical from the inside.

### 2.3 · Exam clashes
동영상콘텐츠 hours may overlap for *classes*; the 수강편람 warns exams may not. Checking needs
per-syllabus reading. 49 of the old top 50 carried at least one such overlap. Never checked,
and a clash is invisible until the exam timetable is published.

### 2.4 · The output is a set, not a policy
On 8/25 he does not "choose a timetable" — he clicks in an order, on 대기순번제, and finds out
what he got. The model emits a ranked set with no click order and no scored fallback chain.
The equal-swap lists are the raw material for one, but they are **unordered**: nothing knows
which alternative is more likely to still have a seat. That ordering is exactly what the 8/14
data would supply (G-5).

### 2.5 · Everything about the future rests on three catalogues
K is measured against 2024/2025/2026 standing in for 2027–2029. Three years of observed drift
is the entire handle we have on four years of extrapolation. The variation is preserved rather
than averaged, which is the best available — but it bounds nothing.

### 2.6 · My own error rate has not gone to zero
Over this one session, self-caught: a sign error on the season bias (R231→R233), a median
reported over a bimodal input (R228→R229), a "fair-share" quota that contradicted the ledger's
own note (R235→R236), and a display that made a genuine duplicate look like a real alternative
(R237). Each was found by Iden asking, or by a check run afterward — **not by the model
noticing.** There is no reason to think the rate is now zero, and the next one will look
exactly as settled as these did before they broke.
